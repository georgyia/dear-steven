## What is sent where

Your question goes to the server running this copy of Dear Steven and from there to OpenAI, which composes the answer from the passages the server found. Answers are requested with OpenAI’s response storage switched off; OpenAI’s own [data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data) apply to everything it receives.

If you ask to be interviewed, your answers go to the server and on to OpenAI, which writes the next question and, at the end, the “What I learned about you” lines, which quote only your own words. The interview isn’t stored on the server: it lives in this tab, and is gone when you start again or close it.

Spoken replies send the answer’s text to OpenAI to turn it into speech. Voice input uses your browser’s own speech recognition where it has one — in some browsers that sends the audio to the browser maker (Google in Chrome, for example), unless on-device recognition is switched on in Voice settings. In browsers without it, the recording goes to the server and on to OpenAI to be transcribed, and is never stored.

The in-drawer video player and the podcast audio load from YouTube and from the podcast’s host only when you press play.

## What is kept

By default the server keeps no record of your questions or answers, and its logs record only that something failed, not what you asked. Whoever runs a copy can switch on an answer log for evaluation (`DEAR_STEVEN_LOG_ANSWERS`), which keeps questions and answers on their server; it is off unless they turn it on. The server also counts today’s estimated OpenAI spend, which says nothing about who asked what, and keeps short-lived rate-limit counters in memory.

The hosting platform a copy runs on may keep its own standard access logs.

## What stays in your browser

Your voice settings and whether you have seen the first-visit notice are kept in this browser only; the mode you chose (Ask, Talk or Interview) is kept in this tab until you close it. If you switch on conversation memory, the conversation is kept in this browser’s storage on this device, until you press Forget or switch it off. If, after an interview, you choose to keep what Dear Steven learned about you, that short text (at most 600 characters, shown to you exactly) is kept in this browser’s storage on this device and sent with your questions, exactly as shown, until you edit it or press Forget; it is never kept on the server. If you choose a language, a cookie remembers it so the page opens in that language. If you paste your own OpenAI key, it is kept in this tab — or on this device, if you ask — and sent only to this site with your requests, which then run on your OpenAI account; the server uses it for that request and never stores or logs it. If you share an answer, the link itself carries your question and the answer — whoever you give it to can read both — and nothing about it is stored on the server; opening the link sends it to the server once, only to check that this copy wrote that answer. If this copy has an access code, a cookie records that you entered it. There are no tracking cookies and no analytics.
