## What was checked, and the sources

Signs of machine-written text that the video's captions, the end card and this copy avoid:

- Em dashes, and middle dots or interpuncts used as separators ("A · B · C").
- Contrast framing: "not X, but Y", "it's not about X, it's about Y", "most X do A, this one does B".
- Lists of three where two or four would be honest, and short slogan fragments ("Fast. Simple. Done.").
- Title Case headings, bold for emphasis, emoji as bullets, curly quotes mixed with straight ones.
- Inflated words: delve, crucial, tapestry, underscore, showcase, seamless, effortless, elevate, unlock, empower, game-changer, supercharge.
- Vague attribution ("experts say"), hedging ("it's worth noting"), rhetorical questions followed by the answer, and a closing summary that restates the piece.
- Colons that join a label to a slogan in a headline ("Dear Steven: answers with proof").

Sources: Wikipedia's field guide "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup), the explainx.ai list of ten signs, Hunting the Muse's "How to spot when writing is AI", and the 2025 coverage of the em dash as a tell (Rolling Stone, NPR). The middle dot came from the owner's own observation; none of the published guides lists it, but it is common in machine-written interface and marketing copy.
